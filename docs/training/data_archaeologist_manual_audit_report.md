# Data Archaeologist — Manual Audit of 18 Flagged Examples

## Executive result

- **18 flagged examples reviewed** against the original prompt and completion.
- **16 need revision** before being treated as gold-standard training targets.
- **2 are broadly usable with caveats** (examples 23 and 76).
- This is a manual quality review of the flagged subset, not validation of all 100 examples.
- Do **not** start AutoScientist training with the current 100-row candidate as if it were validated ground truth.

The most serious recurring defects are: inferring MNAR from observed correlations, inventing causal/root-cause explanations, imputing values without a defensible missingness model, asserting keys from small samples, and recommending irreversible data cleaning without preserving provenance.

## Cross-cutting corrections required

1. **Separate evidence from hypothesis.** A correlation or co-occurrence is evidence of association, not proof of cause, MNAR, a system bug, or a business rule.
2. **Do not infer MNAR casually.** If missingness depends on observed variables, MAR may be plausible; MNAR requires dependence on the unobserved value or explicit sensitivity assumptions. Many prompts do not contain enough evidence to distinguish MAR from MNAR.
3. **Never fabricate identifiers or dates.** Avoid hashing arbitrary row contents into IDs, stripping identifiers down to digits, defaulting year-only dates to January 1, or imputing dates from weak correlations.
4. **Do not delete or overwrite ambiguous data.** Quarantine suspect rows, preserve raw values, add normalized fields and provenance flags, and record the transformation.
5. **Do not infer keys from tiny samples.** Describe candidate keys, then require full-table uniqueness, non-nullness, and referential-integrity checks.
6. **Do not claim statistical significance without a test.** State the test, sample size, statistic, assumptions, and p-value—or remove the significance claim.
7. **Make imputation conditional.** First determine whether nulls mean unknown, not applicable, structurally absent, censored, or invalid. Do not impute structural nulls as ordinary missing values.
8. **Avoid engine-specific claims as universal facts.** Partition pruning, zone maps, sort-key benefits, and pushdown depend on the database and file format.
9. **Preserve uncertainty in the output.** Use labels such as `observed`, `inferred`, `unverified`, and `requires_external_validation`; confidence should be calibrated to evidence.

## Training recommendation

- Keep examples 23 and 76 only after adding explicit caveats about sample size and candidate hypotheses.
- Rewrite the other 16 completions, or exclude them from the first training run.
- For each revised example, require a second-pass check that every conclusion is traceable to prompt evidence and that every recommended action is reversible or explicitly conditional.
- Split the dataset before training. Keep a held-out evaluation set that is never used for prompt/rule tuning or training.
- The existing JSONL is structurally normalized; it is **not** a validated gold dataset. This audit does not certify the remaining 82 examples.

## Important limitation

This audit is based on the synthetic prompt/completion pairs supplied. It does not verify real-world domain policies, source-system logs, or the factual correctness of claims that require external documentation.
